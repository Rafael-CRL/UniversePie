## BATCH 1

### Quiz 1 [discrimination]
**Q:** Choose the correct phrase to complete the sentence: 'After weeks of hard work and many late nights, I finally ____ my project, even though I had a terrible flu for part of it.'
1. got through
2. got it shipped
3. got over
4. came up with

**Explanation:** The phrasal verb 'get through' means to manage to do or complete something, especially something difficult. 'Got it shipped' refers to sending something. 'Got over' means to recover from an illness or difficult experience. 'Came up with' means to suggest or think of an idea.

### Quiz 2 [production]
**Q:** You want to express annoyance because a group of people seems to be complaining simply for the sake of complaining, without a real purpose. Which expression from the cards best fits this communicative intent?
1. just so you could hear yourselves complain?
2. to come up with an excuse?
3. to make the request?
4. to make this a bigger thing than it is?

**Explanation:** The phrase 'just so you could hear yourselves complain?' (Card 6) is used sarcastically to criticize people who are complaining unnecessarily or excessively. 'Come up with an excuse' (Card 1) is about inventing a reason. 'Make the request' (Card 5) is about asking for something. 'Make this a bigger thing than it is' (Card 8) implies exaggeration, but not specifically about the act of complaining.

### Quiz 3 [contextual]
**Q:** During a discussion about allowing minor exceptions to a company rule, a team member says, 'I'm worried this is a slippery slope.' What is the pragmatic implication of this statement?
1. They think the situation is currently fine, but could become problematic if we don't act decisively.
2. They believe that allowing a small exception will inevitably lead to a series of increasingly worse or undesirable consequences.
3. They are suggesting that the rule is difficult to apply consistently and might lead to misunderstandings.
4. They are exaggerating the potential negative impact of the small exception.

**Explanation:** 'Slippery slope' (Card 9) is an idiom used to describe a course of action that, once started, will lead to a chain of undesirable and progressively worse situations or consequences. It's a warning about the long-term, negative progression from a seemingly minor initial step. Option D describes 'making this a bigger thing than it is' (Card 8), which is a different concept.

## BATCH 2

### Quiz 1 [discrimination]
**Q:** After he witnessed the crime, the police told him he should ____ for a while until they caught the suspects.
1. lay low
2. go off the grid
3. bail
4. be out in the open

**Explanation:** 'Lay low' means to stay out of sight and avoid attracting attention temporarily. 'Go off the grid' implies a more permanent and complete disappearance, becoming untraceable. 'Bail' means to leave quickly, often abandoning a commitment. 'Out in the open' is the opposite, meaning to be public or revealed.

### Quiz 2 [production]
**Q:** You and your friend have been keeping a secret from your family. After a lot of stress, you both decide to tell them everything. Which expression best conveys your relief that the truth is now known and no longer hidden?
1. "I'm glad it's out in the open."
2. "I'm glad we're bailing now."
3. "I'm glad we can lay low."
4. "I'm glad it's snug."

**Explanation:** 'Out in the open' means something is no longer hidden or secret, but openly acknowledged. This fits the feeling of relief after revealing a secret. 'Bailing' means leaving quickly, 'lay low' means hiding, and 'snug' means comfortable and cozy.

### Quiz 3 [interference]
**Q:** A Brazilian Portuguese speaker might mistakenly translate 'when it comes to money' literally. What does the English phrase 'when it comes to money' actually mean?
1. Regarding money, or in matters concerning money.
2. When the money arrives.
3. If money is available.
4. Because of money.

**Explanation:** 'When it comes to' is an idiomatic phrase meaning 'in connection with,' 'regarding,' or 'as far as X is concerned.' It's commonly confused with a literal translation of 'quando vem para' by Portuguese speakers, which would imply arrival or movement.

## BATCH 3

### Quiz 1 [discrimination]
**Q:** After the scandal, the politician decided to ________ for a while, staying out of the public eye and avoiding any media appearances, but he didn't completely disappear from society.
1. lay low
2. go off the grid
3. slink away
4. take a hint

**Explanation:** 'Lay low' means to stay hidden and avoid attention, which perfectly fits the context of avoiding media without completely vanishing. 'Go off the grid' implies a more radical disappearance, becoming untraceable. 'Slink away' suggests moving secretly or shamefully. 'Take a hint' means to understand an indirect suggestion.

### Quiz 2 [polysemy]
**Q:** In which sentence does 'come up' mean 'to arise or become a topic for discussion'?
1. I'm sure the issue will come up in the meeting.
2. We quickly came up with a solution to the problem.
3. My favorite song came up on the radio.
4. She came up to me and asked for directions.

**Explanation:** In option A, 'come up' means that an issue will be mentioned or discussed. Options B, C, and D represent other common meanings or phrasal verbs: 'come up with' (to invent/produce), 'came up' (appeared/was played), and 'came up to' (approached).

### Quiz 3 [interference]
**Q:** You're organizing a reunion for your old university friends. Which phrase would you use to express the idea of everyone gathering again, emphasizing it's not about two people reconciling?
1. Let's all get back together!
2. Let's all reconcile!
3. Vamos reatar todos!
4. Let's make up!

**Explanation:** 'Get back together' is the correct phrase for a group reuniting. The Portuguese 'reatar' (Option C) is a common interference trap, as it typically refers to a romantic reconciliation, not simply gathering a group. 'Reconcile' and 'make up' also imply resolving differences or an argument, usually between individuals.

## BATCH 4

### Quiz 1 [polysemy]
**Q:** The word "sound" has several distinct meanings. In which of the following sentences does "sound" mean "in good condition," "reliable," or "free from defects"?
1. A) The old bridge was reported to be sound despite its age.
2. B) A strange sound echoed from the empty room.
3. C) That sounds like a perfect plan for our trip.
4. D) She experienced a sound sleep after a long day of travel.

**Explanation:** In option A, 'sound' means the bridge is structurally safe and in good condition. Options B, C, and D use 'sound' to mean a noise, to appear/seem, and deep/uninterrupted, respectively.


---

### Quiz 2 [interference]
**Q:** Imagine a situation where a student witnesses their friend cheating on an exam. They are considering telling the teacher about it. Which expression correctly describes the action of reporting their friend to the authority (the teacher), while avoiding a common L1 trap for Portuguese speakers?
1. A) The student is thinking about turning their friend in.
2. B) The student is thinking about delivering their friend.
3. C) The student is thinking about sticking up for their friend.
4. D) The student is thinking about pranking their friend.

**Explanation:** To 'turn someone in' means to report them to an authority, like the police or a teacher. Option B, 'deliver their friend,' is a common literal translation trap from Portuguese 'entregar,' which doesn't carry the meaning of reporting to authorities in English. 'Stick up for' (C) means to defend, and 'prank' (D) means to play a trick on someone.

### Quiz 3 [production]
**Q:** Your boss approaches you asking if you can take on an additional, demanding project, but you already have too much on your plate and feel completely overloaded with current tasks. Which of the following expressions best conveys your current state of being extremely busy and overwhelmed?
1. A) "I'm swamped right now."
2. B) "I'm reported to be busy."
3. C) "I'm known for being busy."
4. D) "I'm wandering with tasks."

**Explanation:** 'Swamped' is the most idiomatic and precise way to express being overwhelmed with a large amount of work. While 'reported to be busy' (B) or 'known for being busy' (C) imply busyness, they don't capture the feeling of being completely overloaded. Option D is an awkward, non-idiomatic construction.
